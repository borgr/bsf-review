# Abstract

**Drought memory in sediment microbiomes** — Application No. XXXX — A. Levi (Weizmann Institute of Science); B. Jones (University of Michigan)

Microbial communities in river sediments experience repeated drought, and how they respond is poorly understood. This project will sample sediments at four sites across a precipitation gradient, extract and sequence DNA from every core, and use standard community-ecology pipelines to describe how composition changes during and after each drought. We will compare sites with long and short drought histories, measure recovery times, and relate them to environmental covariates such as temperature, organic carbon and grain size. Sampling will take place over three seasons in each country, with cores processed in both laboratories under a shared protocol. The resulting data will be released publicly, together with code for all analyses, and the sampling design will be documented so that other groups can extend it to further sites. We expect to describe the trajectories of community composition across drought cycles in unprecedented detail, and to characterise which taxa persist, which disappear, and which return after rewetting. Data will be collected from sites that differ in climate and land use, and multiple seasons will be sampled at each site to capture variation between years. Both laboratories will take part in fieldwork and analysis. The work will be of interest to microbial ecologists, hydrologists and environmental managers concerned with river health under a changing climate, and to modellers who need parameters for sediment processes. Training of students in both countries is an integral part of the project, and joint workshops will be held annually.
